The internal `Version` constant stays "5" from v2 through v7 (`main.go:15`/`:27`). Live 11-26: unreleased v12 is #2 at 0.346.

Constants across all versions:
- 6 waves and 760 chatter pairs (`world.go:13-18`; `"chatter":760` in every `mix.go`).
- Chatter deliberately names cast members by full name, first name or nickname "so a search by name finds many hits without an answer" (v3 `chatter.go:8-9`).

### (a) Case shapes

**v1: five families, all two-item lists with no count word** (`querverweis-v1 traps.go:58-175`, README table)
- `churn`: "Which gyms does Clara Moss work out at these days?" Two places under the full name. Traps: a place she later gave up, told without a cessation keyword ("has written it off", `categories.go:29-30`), and a same-surname relative's place.
- `title`: one item under the full name, one only under "Ms. Moss" (`traps.go:77-94`). Trap: a relative with the same surname and the other title; at m2 also a given-up place.
- `back`: one place given up and later taken up again, under "Ms. Surname" (`traps.go:98-113`).
- `handover`: "Which cafés does my physiotherapist get coffee at now?" The role passed from A to B. Gold is B's places, one of them under title; traps are A's places and a place B gave up (`traps.go:118-145`).
- `twin`: "Which bookshops does my dentist Clara buy books at?" A namesake with the same first name owns the distractors (`traps.go:151-175`).

**v2–v3: weighted "Bausteine"** (`baustein.go:13-37`)
- A `liste` block is built from three knobs:
  - Frage (question form): full name, role, handover, namesake, nickname, title, relation.
  - Zugang (2–4 items): full name, title, nickname, initial, return, correction, swap (*ersatz*), anchor, feature, pronoun, confirmed, taken over.
  - Fallen: 20 trap kinds.
- New trap modules, all unused in the shipped mixes:
  - `baustein_gesichert.go`: a hunch confirmed or refuted later, a retraction ("forget what I told you … I made that up"), a hypothetical ("If Clara takes the job, P will be her gym").
  - `baustein_pronomen.go`: an opposite-sex sibling companion, with the item only in the pronoun sentence.
  - `baustein_wem.go`: ownership direction ("Tom passed his loyalty card for P on to Clara") and co-occurrence ("Clara keeps telling me Y is Tom's favourite gym").
- Shipped mixes:
  - v2 (`mix.go`, candidate qv2fd): `b035` full + *ersatz* ("Clara switched her gym from Y to P"; Y is the distractor) at 0.25; `b038` four items at 0.35; four v1 families at 0.1 each.
  - v3 (candidate qv3b): five 2-item liste blocks. Each block has one "Ms. Surname" item plus one item told by correction, swap or return. Traps are abkehr (giving up) plus a relative-with-title or a vowel-variant nickname. The role share is 2.4/4.4 = 0.545, just under the `LoadMix` cap of 0.55 (`baustein.go:122-127`).

**v4–v5: akte (file)** (`block_akte.go`)
- Example: "Which two things are going to Clara Moss?" (`question_type` "routed-items").
- Evidence is a chain: "Delivery K-472 is the one going to Clara Moss" → "Box 2468 goes on delivery K-472" → "The red lamp is packed in box 2468". A loose item on the delivery is the 2-hop item.
- *ruecknahme*: a box is added and then pulled in a later wave.
- Background deliveries fill the distractors up to 8 (`:247-252`).
- v5 adds *zahlendreher* (a box number with two adjacent digits swapped on a background delivery), *losweg* (a loose item later scratched), and *hglose* (`block_akte.go:55-59, 156, 302-330`).

**v6–v7: buchung (bookings)** (`block_buchung.go:195-315`)
- Example: "Which of the bookings for Clara Moss's quiz night still stand?" (`question_type` "bookings-standing").
- Three venues:
  - R is booked, cancelled, then reinstated ("found a slot after all, so our booking … stands again").
  - C is booked, then cancelled; it is the distractor.
  - U is hidden: "The X is booked as well, for the do on Monday the 3rd" (`:97-100`), joined to the person only through a date bridge ("Clara's do is on Monday the 3rd", templates near v7 `block_buchung.go:128-131`).
- *gemischt*: the six booking events fall in six distinct waves (`:444-459`), so state must be computed in time order.
- *alias*: C is cancelled only under a descriptive alias ("the red-brick barn on Tollgate Lane").
- v7 *sperre1* (`block_sperre.go:129-272`):
  - The date bridge is replaced by a pronoun chain. An intro pair ties the person to a role and a place (a stall, a business). A following pair says "{N} told me {subj} has {pos} summer party on D" in pronoun form (`:311`, `sperreTagVP` `:221`).
  - Rival candidates' bookings are distractors (`:257-270`).
  - *sichtbar* (`:254-256`) prefixes the event name in every booking sentence. This lets R/C, and U through its date, be matched without the pronoun chain: a difficulty-down knob.
- Gold is always [U, R], with one or more distractors. Evidence is 8 pairs, the validator cap.
- Dormant in v7: *falls* (single-value plan-condition "if D cancels we use F"), *rest* (shortlist elimination), *koref* (two-part question: gym by pronoun + current phone provider after a chain of changes, or a relative date like "a week after her driving test"), and standalone *sperre*.

### (b) Weaknesses targeted
- v1–v3:
  - Name-variant resolution: title + surname, first name, nickname, role, namesake.
  - Same-surname relatives with the other title.
  - Given-up places with no cessation keyword, which defeats "no longer / stopped" heuristics.
  - Previous role holder.
  - Swaps and corrections.
- v4–v5:
  - ID-chain joins (person ← delivery ← box ← item) with removals and digit-transposed look-alike IDs.
- v6–v7:
  - Latest-state tracking over book / cancel / reinstate.
  - Joining via a date or an alias.
  - Pronoun coreference across adjacent pairs.
- Throughout, person-name retrieval is starved:
  - name-heavy chatter;
  - in v7, booking sentences never name the person.

### (c) Version changes and reasons
- **v1** design notes record panel results (`traps.go:7-21`):
  - Value questions were "either trivial or unsolved".
  - Two-item lists with a silently given-up place split the panel.
  - The count word "two" was removed because "a harness that found one place filled up with the trap (title-m0 0.35, churn-m0 0.5 vs 0.8 without the count)".
  - Twin's given-up-under-title variant was dropped because "almost nobody solved it" (`traps.go:147-150`).
  - handover gained a given-up trap because "without this trap everyone found exactly one place, trivial" (`traps.go:115-117`).
- **v2/v3:** a mix optimizer (`eigen_optimierer.py`, named in the `mix.go` header) picks block weights. v3 drops the v1 families and makes every block "Ms. Surname + correction/swap/return". Our 11-16 note records v3 as pinned in all 7 revealed seasons, with leaders at 0.80–0.92.
- **v4:**
  - Wholesale rename (baustein → block); every comment, the README rationale and all tests are deleted.
  - An answerability fix binds the title explicitly ("When I say Ms. Kelwick, I mean Ms. Clara Kelwick", `block.go:343, 602-636`). It is dormant, because liste is no longer emitted.
  - The family switches to akte, and the count word "two" returns.
  - Apparent reason: leaders had learned the v3 name-variant shapes (we reached 0.91 locally on 11-16).
- **v5:** weight 2.0 on the trap-heaviest akte block `z6c` (ruecknahme + zahlendreher + losweg), against 0.5 each for the older blocks.
- **v6:** akte deleted; bookings-standing introduced (falls and rest are built but not emitted). The target moves from ID chains to state over time.
- **v7:** the pronoun bridge (sperre1) plus *sichtbar*, *ohneladen* and *frageort*. *sichtbar* appears to soften the pronoun step. A check message cites "g14_anlegen", suggesting internal builds are ahead of the live v12.

### (d) Difficulty tuning
- Always one reachable item and one hidden item, so "found one" scores 0.5, which counts as solved without being trivial:
  - v1 full name vs title;
  - v4/v5 the 2-hop loose item vs the 3-hop box item;
  - v6/v7 the reinstated R vs the date-only U.
- m0–m2 levels rotate per case in v1 (`main.go:56`).
- From v2 on, weights come from an optimizer run against a local panel, and the role share is held just under the 0.55 cap.
- Each pack is single-shape from v4 on (two variants at 1:1), so discrimination rests on per-variant hit rates.

### (e) Scorer-aware or gaming aspects
- The design is explicitly built around the 0.5 solve line: "one place = 0.5, solved, mean stays low" (`traps.go:9-17`).
- The count word is toggled to provoke list padding:
  - removed in v1–v3 because it made harnesses add the trap;
  - restored in akte ("Which two things…").
- Distractors use present-tense assertive templates, tested against the validator's past/negation lexicon (v3 `main_test.go:272-300`). So a memory dump counts as asserting them, and harness supersession keywords do not fire.
- Up to 8 distractors per akte case.
- Non-standard `question_type` labels ("routed-items", "bookings-standing").
- No unanswerable cases were found: first names, dates and aliases are pack-unique.
- From v4 on, all design rationale is stripped from the release.

Live: dan0l v1 was #1 on 11-17 at 0.309; bujhmml v9 (unreleased) is #1 at 0.396.

Code:
- Original, not a starter-kit fork: the diff against the starter kit is about 1,500 lines, with its own `builder.go`, `refs.go` and `lines.go`.
- It uses the vendor datagen only for types (`main.go:9`, `builder.go:9-10`).
- Name "dittogen" is unrelated to our `ditto-generator` ("horizon").

### (a) Case shape
- One family only: a person-centred list via `listCase` (`dan0l-v1 families.go:64-116`).
- Example, dan0l v1: "What are the two dentists Freya Lindqvist goes to these days?"
  - Item A is told under a reference form, e.g. a married name:
    - "Freya Lindqvist got married in the summer and goes by Freya Okafor now."
    - "Freya Okafor goes to Bramble Dentist and swears by it." (`refs.go:291-304`, `families.go:33-46`)
  - Item B is told under a two-hop relational description, e.g. "the brother of my friend who ran the Berlin marathon":
    - an achievement record ties the friend r ("Tom Reed ran the Berlin marathon last spring");
    - a tie record makes p r's only sibling, flatmate or business partner;
    - the item is told under that description (`refs.go:338-377`, ties `:193-218`, achievements `:95-124`).
- Reference forms (`refs.go:10-26`):
  - full, first, handle (username), typo, pronoun (item told in the same session after "Caught up with X"), nick, relation, attr, married, role, title, event ("the woman I met at the cheese festival");
  - sibling chains: sibnick, sibevent, sibattr; v2 adds sibrole, sibfavour, sibpet, sibcar, sibband; v3 adds sibalias.
- The `question_type` encodes the forms, e.g. "ref.title.sibpet" (`families.go:106`; v3 `:104`).

### (b) Traps
- **Stale place:** "used to go … stopped after it changed owners" (`families.go:81-84`).
- **Never-visited lure:** "keeps saying she'll check out X, hasn't been yet" (`:85-89`).
- **Relational anchor's own place:** the friend, sibling or flatmate r also has a place of the same kind, stated plainly under r's full name, which is a strong co-occurrence decoy (`:91-98`).
- **Another person's place:** via `otherValue`.
- **Distractor padding:** `widenDistractors` (`:118-149`; v3 `:198`) fills to `challenge.MaxDistractors` with every other venue of the same category, so naming any same-kind place zeroes the case.
- **What it tests:** coreference through descriptions (married name, title, handle, event, achievement) and two-hop relational chains, plus present vs past vs intended.
- **Haystack is small and name-dense:** 110–120 cast members with 1–3 chatter lines each, plus 12 self lines (`main.go:49-58`); each record is its own session (`builder.go:213-215`).

### (c) Version changes
- **dan0l-v1 → b5835** (`bj1-2.diff`):
  - The a-form weights move toward title. Before: married ×4, title ×4, first ×2. After: title ×7, married ×2, first ×1 (`families.go:173`).
  - Five new sibling-chain anchors: role, favour ("the sister of the man who lent me the ladder"), pet ("the brother of Pretzel's owner"), car, band (`refs.go:265-301`).
  - New "cousin", "neighbour" and "twin" ties (`refs.go:179-203`).
  - More stale and lure phrasings, including "These days X talks about Y all the time, but has never actually been" (`families.go:42-54`). The lure deliberately reuses the question's "these days".
  - Question template variety (`:34-40`).
  - The pronoun form now also lists its intro pair as evidence (`refs.go:233-239`).
  - Reason: more surface variety so agents cannot pattern-match a handful of templates, and "these days" lures that collide lexically with the question.
- **b5835 → 7d2dc** (`bj2-3.diff`), the big change:
  - `listSpec` becomes groups, and every case draws **four items**, one each from `easyForms` (title ×3, first), `farForms` (attr, event, relation, married), `midForms` (nick, handle, role, pronoun) and `bForms` (sibling chains, with sibalias/sibpet/sibrole doubled) (`main.go:47-49`, `families.go:231-237`).
  - The question drops the count: "Which dentists does X go to these days?" (`families.go:138-144`).
  - **No item is ever told under the person's full name.** Even the easy item is "Ms. Lindqvist" or the first name.
  - Uniqueness guards: first-name references are allowed only if no other cast member shares the first name (`refs.go:335-339`), and the sibling/relative bookkeeping is tightened (`:232, 318-320`). cast 110 → 120.
  - Prepared but switched off: `lureFreshPct = 0`, `staleFreshPct = 0` (`families.go:146-149`), with "fresh" stale and lure templates such as "on the waiting list at X since the summer".

### (d) Difficulty tuning
- v1/v2: two items, one easy and one hard, the usual 0.5 design.
- v3: four items with no count. 0.5 needs two of the four, e.g. the title item plus one mid or far item. Graded tiers (easy, mid, far, sibling-chain) spread the panel over 0.25 / 0.5 / 0.75 / 1, and the count-free question removes the "stop at two" cue.
- Every distractor list is padded to the maximum, which makes over-listing fatal.

### (e) Gaming aspects
- `widenDistractors` declares every other same-category venue in the pack as a distractor. That is fair against dumping, but it means one wrong guess zeroes the case.
- The lure templates are written to share "these days" with the question (a retrieval trap rather than a memory test).
- Otherwise the cases are answerable from the evidence and the facts are unique; no grader-quirk exploit was found.

    - "Julia Braskham has joined the Wednesday crew" (`:231`)
    - plus "Julia has a different arrangement with the riverside lot: she goes to the clay studio Q on her own" (`:304-307`). The riverside lot's own place is the decoy.

### (b) Traps
- One-letter nickname look-alikes. In v3 these belong to a same-surname relative ("Bel (Sean)" vs a near-identical nickname).
- The user's own place vs a shared "we" place.
- Near misses.
- Old vs new places.
- Handover (old holder).
- Pronoun next-turn statements.
- From v5 on:
  - group membership indirection, where nothing is stated under the name;
  - conditional plans resolved by a later note;
  - relational "with A's group rather than B's";
  - relative recency ("older membership").
- Banned surface cues in v5 sentences: "except", "instead", "old", or a leading "no" (README v5.0).

### (c) Version changes, with the authors' stated reasons (README)
- **v2.x → v3:** every two-item list "with only the plain place scored 0.5 … those cases were trivial for every agent and earned nothing". v3 asks for three places, so the plain place alone scores 1/3 and fails, and 2 of 3 passes (README v3, `families.go:181-194`).
- **v3.1:** the three-item Handover made agents resolve "my <role>" to the old holder, "which zeroed every item", so it was restored to two items.
- **v3.2:** Lately's other new place is made *undeclared*, so naming it "only earns nothing" (two Lately cases had been zeroed by it).
- **v3.3:** first live round 26140: "14 of 70 cases unsolved (20%)", mostly Together and Signoff. The nearer items now carry the first name ("Mira and I…", "Bel (Sean)").
- **v3.4:** Signoff with the first name every time was "six of six trivial". Even cases now carry the first name and odd cases do not (`16a80a6c families.go:164-175`).
- **v5.0/5.2:** new Arrangement and Settled; Together dropped because "nearly every agent solved" it.
- **v5.5:** "In a scored round the live panel solved half of version 5.2's cases (35 of 70 trivial, 3 unsolved): every note named the group and the place outright." Order and Along name the group only indirectly.
- **v5.6:** "Along split a stronger agent from weaker ones most (a stronger configuration solved 57% of Along cases, our standard agents about 32%), while Order stayed hard for every configuration." Order is out.
- **v5.6.1:** Along's person note is moved after both friends' membership notes.
- **Pool hygiene:**
  - Replacing words shared with other releases (e.g. five words also used by "g118fold"; README v5.5).
  - An automated overlap check reports "0 pool entries, 0 five-word sequences against 40 released archives" (README v5.2).
  - This is defence against copy holds.

### (d) Difficulty tuning
- The most explicit of the five families:
  - list size is chosen so that one item fails and two pass (3-item lists in v3);
  - two-item lists with both items behind a step (v5), so zero steps = 0, one step = 0.5, two steps = 1;
  - families are cut from the rotation when they turn trivial (Together, Afterwards) or unsolved (Order, Nearly's positional variant, Backwards' mishap variant).
- Tests assert the grade thresholds per family with the production grader (README "Checks": "any one item of a three-item case scores below 0.5, two of three score at least 0.5…"; `TestNoStatedItem`).
- Generation skips self-validation because validation "can take tens of seconds on a few [packs]" and counts against the time limit (`main.go` comment, `g1-2.diff:762-764`).

### (e) Gaming aspects
- Strongly grader-engineered list sizes, and undeclared look-alikes chosen so that a near miss costs credit without zeroing.
- Otherwise these are legitimate memory and reasoning tests.
- The low chatter volume makes retrieval easy; difficulty is in reading and chaining, not finding.

### (b) Traps
- Given-up places (explicit).
- A stale weekly booking stated "in the words of a current place", retired only by a later record that does not name it. This is temporal ordering via the weekday slot.
- Group indirection: the group's place is stated without the person.

### (c) Version changes and reasons
- **v2 → v5c:**
  - The nickname and rename paths are dropped; moved booking and a second group are added.
  - The question asks for two of four.
  - README: "Two correct places score 0.5: the direct place and the place the booking was moved to are both recorded under the person's full name, so half marks need no hard retrieval, only the right reading of which booking is current."
  - Reason: v2 needed a hard path for 0.5 (direct alone = 0.25, below the solve line), which risked unsolved cases. v5c makes 0.5 reachable from full-name records, with the stale booking as the zeroing trap.
- **v5c → v7:** the named-move variant and the easiest move sentence are removed, i.e. difficulty is levelled and nudged up after the authors' "judges" (a local LLM panel) found variance between forms.

### (d) Difficulty tuning
- The solve line is reached with two full-name items (direct + moved booking); the zero comes from reading the stale booking as current.
- Full credit needs both group paths too.
- Variant weights and template pruning come from local judge runs (`text.go:101-104`).

### (e) Gaming aspects (strongest flag in this group)
- **Question/gold mismatch.** v5c and v7 ask for "two" places but declare **four** `AnswerItems`, and the grader divides hits by `len(AnswerItems)` (vendor `grade/v13.go:997`).
  - An agent that answers the question as asked, with two correct places, can never score above 0.5.
  - Only agents that ignore the count and list everything reach 0.75–1.
  - This caps compliant agents at the solve line, so cases are "solved but not trivial" with high credit (1 − mean). It is a grader-arithmetic exploit, not a memory test.
- The 2-user interleaved layout with per-case `UserID` is legitimate but uncommon; it halves the per-user haystack.
- Distractors are few (given-up + stale booking); the dump guard is 6.

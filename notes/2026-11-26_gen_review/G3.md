### 1.2 How the banks were made (offline LLM: yes, for the shards; very likely for the worlds)

- `tools/shard_prompt.md:1-13` is an **LLM prompt template**: "Note shard prompt (one call = one world x one frame x ~100
  notes)", "Return the full requested count of notes in every reply", "Output: JSON {...}, nothing
  else". The slots are `{M}` (matter) and `{V}` (value), with frames counsel/deposition/filing/settlement/retainer.
  `shards/court.json` (1,790 notes) and `shards/lease.json` (1,494 notes) follow its output schema
  (`{"world","notes":[{"frame","text","states_value"}]}`). `tools/bank_merge.py:1-7` merges such shards with
  5-gram near-twin dedup ("T1695, paper Q2"). Its docstrings say "Free, no model call", which means the merge and
  check scripts are free; the shards themselves are the model output. `data/pilot.json` is the lease shard's
  1,494 texts (identical list). The court shard's wording has typical model tells, such as curly apostrophes and
  "requires immediate review".
- `data/legal.json` (8 legal worlds, openers, closers, role facts, 61 fillers) is written by
  `tools/build_bank.py:1` ("from the hand-written banks (run once, offline)") from `tools/bank_worlds.py`,
  `tools/extra_lease.py`, `tools/extra_roles.py` ("Extra hand-written forms ... (T1658, D498)"). They are labelled
  hand-written, but the task tags (T1658, D498, "paper section 9") show an agent-run workflow. That they are
  hand-written cannot be verified.
- `data/world{1,2,3}.json`: three persona worlds (vet clinic owner, event planner, small landlord). Each has
  350 free-prose notes (mean 545-666 chars, max ~12.8 k) and 1,822 / 1,915 / 1,925 graded questions. The
  `knobs` blocks (`target_notes 350`, `note_mean_chars_target 590`, `out_of_order_share_target 0.22`,
  `look_alike_pairs`, `hub_mention_target 25`) and the casual voice of the notes ("notes from the drive
  ... ugh ... ticket in the glovebox") point to spec-driven LLM authoring. The `note` field reads "world1 minus
  twin_no_detail, sum_derived, ungrounded; generator upload v1; plus code-templated pool cases (v3h-pool
  11-11)". So a hand-made (probably LLM-made) core plus a large code-templated pool: `as_of` is 1,126-1,336 per world
  and `renamed_entity` 436-637. The authoring scripts (`pool/messy.py`, named in `main.go:59-60`) are
  **not shipped**.
- **None of the LLM material is used by the shipped gen-v8 or gen-v7 packs.** gen-v8's `release.go:8-14`

## 6. Cross-family

**Do big or data-heavy generators beat small logic ones?** No.
- The data-heaviest submissions (Dr's lineage, 2.2-2.9 MB of LLM-made banks) score 0.08-0.24 when the bank is
  used (v3h 0.06-0.19, split 0.09-0.24) and 0 for the hop column. In gen-v8 and gen-v7 the banks are not even
  drawn.
- reaxgen and its clones carry 0.87 MB of name/city tables and peaked early (0.30-0.45), then decayed to
  under 0.07 as agents learned the nickname-binding and stale-copy shapes.
- The strongest released generator here is gan-v1, which is pure logic (18 k lines plus 21 k test lines, no data)
  and holds 0.15-0.27 across versions. Size there is verification machinery (solvers, wrong-reader checks,
  oracle checks), not content.
- sn118gen's size is vendored public code. Re-serving official datagen cases scored 0.02-0.06.
- The current live leader, gen118 v3 (0.318), is unreleased, so its type is unknown.

**Offline LLM-authored material.** Only Dr's lineage, and there it is unambiguous for the legal note shards
(`tools/shard_prompt.md`, `shards/*.json`) and very likely for the v3h worlds. The other generators are template
code. reaxgen's tables are public census/GeoNames data copied from datagen.

**Code sharing across hotkeys.**
- Dr runs 5HKmBL1H, 5GsKtwcq and 5Dsrh27d on three coldkeys. The proof is Dr's hotkey hard-coded in 5GsKtwcq's test,
  plus private code uploaded before any release.
- The reaxgen template runs on 5DaLyZfV, 5CXEMm6u and 5ELk1RHE on three coldkeys. The evidence is the "reaxgen" README, the
  `b1f1`/`b1f2` salts, and the operator's queue test, which encodes their archive sizes.
- In both lineages the copies differ just enough (salt, case kind) to pass the identical-tree and probe-seed copy checks.
  The operator's response so far is the challenger-queue "template family" interleaving, not rejection.

**Direction of travel.**
1. Convergence on **partial-credit lists**: one easy item, one or two items behind an identity or event bridge, and a
   look-alike or stale decoy that zeroes the answer. Examples: reaxgen ls2b, sn118gen V7, gan list3v2 (3 items, 2 to
   solve), Dr's trap lists (4 dates, 2 to solve).
2. **Harness-specific targeting**, measured on local copies of released agents: reaxgen's window and fan-out cells, sn118gen V6's
   "went back to" rule, Dr's 600-char split. The memory test increasingly encodes another miner's
   implementation detail.
3. **Frontier-lane plays**: gen-v8 is built to be panel-unsolved and oracle-solved, and gan v4 adds
   `OracleCheckAll`. These columns depend entirely on the oracle; when it was down (26148-26152, 26172-26176)
   they scored 0.
4. **Heavier self-verification** (solvers, generic wrong readers, dump sweeps, OracleContext checks) to stay
   inside the unsolved/trivial band.
5. **Decay.** The single-template shapes lost most of their credit within days of being scored: the reaxgen family
   went from 0.30-0.45 to 0.01-0.06, split from 0.24 to 0.09, and trap lists from 0.13 to 0.05. gan-v1, with a
   wider multi-family mix, held best (0.15-0.27). This rewards rapid re-release and hotkey multiplication over
   durable family design.

# G2: generator review (wrengen2, tern-gen, tessera, sn118-recall, lh2gen, kinlist, dittogen)

Date 2026-11-26. Sources were read only, from `/home/bittensor/ditto/.cache/miner_gens/dl/<label>/src` (written below as
`<label>:<file>:<line>`). Nothing was built or run. `vendor/` was ignored. The baseline is the public starter kit
(`repos/ditto-subnet/miners/dittobench-generator-starter-kit`, families Default + Hardened).

Scoring reminder: a case is solved at a score of 0.5 or more. It needs at least 2 solver owners, or it counts as
unsolved and the pack is multiplied by (1 - unsolved share)^2. A case every agent solves is trivial. Credit is
(1 - mean), x1.5 when the champion fails. So every family below tries to give the median agent exactly 0.5 and
rarely 1.0.

## 1. wrengen2 (5D2qgKgG; same owner as agent wren-mem). Live v11 0.173 (rounds 0.15-0.37)

Seven releases, from 11-18 to 11-26. Internal versions, from `main.go` `Version`: 16 -> 23 -> 30 -> 33 -> 39 -> 44 -> 48b.

### (a) Case shapes
- **Trap3** (v16-v44): "Which bakeries does Holly Brennan buy bread at these days?"
  - Sure item: stated under the full name, plus a later "still going there, same as ever" record.
  - Hard item: one record under the title and surname ("Ms. Brennan swears by the bakery next to the clock tower on
    Mill Lane"). A separate record with no person in it names the bakery next to that landmark.
  - Lures: a relative with the same surname and the other title, who has their own place; a place the person dropped
    ("gave X one last chance in the spring", "walks straight past X now"), or only planned to try.
  - Source: `wrengen2-v2_4584b510:fam/trap3.go:69-109`, frames in `fam/mine.go:213-254`.
- **Month3** (v16-v39): "Which restaurants did Corin and I go to in March?"
  - Gold 1 is a meal told in March.
  - Gold 2 is told in April as "back in March ... the restaurant next to <landmark>", plus a bridge record.
  - Lures: a February meal told in March, an April meal, and a March booking that fell through.
  - Each wave is one calendar month (`fam/builder.go:23-31`, Waves=5). Source: `fam/month3.go:31-53`.
- **Window** (v44 onward; the only family from v48b): the Month3 question asked about March only.
  - Gold 1: "Corin and I had dinner at X tonight" (`wrengen2_36b292d3:fam/window.go:82-87`).
  - Gold 2 is told only by the companion's spouse, who never names their partner: "<spouse> said their other half
    keeps bringing up the dinner the two of us had at Y in March". One introduction record ties the spouse to the
    companion (`window.go:139-148`).
  - Lures (`window.go:89-136`):
    - a meal in the month before;
    - a meal in the month after;
    - a cancelled table;
    - a booking made in March for April, then eaten;
    - a "looking back" note two months later;
    - walked past the restaurant without going in;
    - a meal there with someone else while the companion was ill.

### (b) Weaknesses targeted
- Binding by title and surname, and look-alike relatives with the same surname.
- Implicit cessation (no stop keyword).
- Two-hop bridges through landmarks, and from v23 a split "deep hop": "<owner> took over the bakery next to <landmark>"
  plus "<owner> named the place X" (`96fc8a66:fam/trap3.go:138-140`, `deepHop`).
- From v39, a spouse who refers to their partner only as "their other half" (`1013d222:fam/hide.go:5-16`).
- Event date versus record date (Window), and whether the companion was actually at the meal.

### (c) Version history

  block "Code-backed retrieval traps (rival lineage read as text, 11-17)" (`trap3.go:110`), for example `Trap3FreqZShare`:
  the dropped place is named in 18 neutral records, because "lineages ... stop following a key held by more than 12-16

- **v30** (e5df75da). Adds `relay.go` (a numbered chain request -> supplier list -> rota -> confirmation slip -> called
  off) and `sharp.go` (slot homes, twist, same-title, short-form nickname lures). All of these default to 0, and
  `Trap3SureRoutes` is nil (`trap3.go:229-232`), so the output is essentially unchanged from v23.
- **v33** (2457bd4a). The first real dial change:
  - Trap3 profiles 0.45 "one last chance", 0.20 planned plus last chance, 0.20 "walks past", 0.15 planned plus walks
    past (`fam/trap3.go:256`);
  - asked-month weights 0.2/0.5/0.3 (`fam/month3.go:45`);

### (d) Difficulty tuning
- Every case is a two-item list with an easy item worth 0.5, so a case is solved unless the agent takes a lure.
- Unsolved cases are avoided by keeping lures to ones the fits say "bite" moderately. For example, "looking back
  always-on raises unsolved cases on lh2/dinn-type panels" (`36b292d3:main.go:71`).
- The trivial rate is driven to zero by making the second item reachable only through the spouse hop.
- The tuning is fitted per lineage on the owner's own live-label dataset.

### (e) Scorer-gaming signals
- **Panel fitting.** Dials, and even sentence templates, are fitted by regression to the live panel's per-agent zero
  rates for named agent lineages. That optimises the column against today's 5-agent panel more than it tests a general
  memory skill.
- **Half-cap by design.** Gold 2 is deliberately routed so that most agents stop at 0.5.
- **Ambiguous evidence.** "The dinner the two of us had" in the spouse's report (`window.go:145-148`) is meant as "the
  other half and the user". A literal reader can take "the two of us" to mean the spouse and their partner, which makes
  gold 2 arguable.
- **Dump guards.** v30's slot option (`sharp.go:8-15`, `builder.go:311`) puts the other homes' places into DumpGuard, so
  listing every home's place scores 0. This turns the dump guard into a lure. It is off by default.
- **No pack self-check.** The generator has no `ValidateGenerated` self-check (`main.go` `run`).

### (f) Shared code and borrowing
- Wrengen2 has its own `fam` package; it is not a starter-kit fork apart from the contract skeleton.
- About 600 lines of dead families are kept (Leftover, Overlap, Triad, Swap, RoleWhen, Employer, Seats, Home in
  `fam/families.go`, plus Trap, Near, Trap2, Overlap2, Month2, Spread in `fam/mine.go`).
- The relay chain (v30, `relay.go:5-16`) has the same structure as tern-gen v1's relay family (numbered hand-offs and
  an unnamed cancellation, released 11-18). The "rival lineage read as text, 11-17" note suggests wrengen read rival
  sources.
- In the other direction, memgen v9 (5Hb5Sxe4, released 11-25) contains wrengen's lure sentence almost verbatim ("gave
  X one last chance in the spring and was not impressed", `memgen-v9_49a527a1:gen/v10mix.go:227`, compare
  `wrengen2_96fc8a66:fam/trap3.go` `trap3LastChanceGeneric`).

  - Four or five lots are keyed by number. Every other lot has a change, or a filing that took effect. The answer lot

  - Decoy: a twin lot with swapped digits (`families/unch.go:5-21, 229-246`).

**v13/v14** (fee39692 11-24, v6 8feec515 11-26; the two are identical apart from the version string). The single
family is "stand-in beds" (`families/stand.go:13-20`).
- "Which two beds does Odile Hartwell look after at the moment?"
- Sure item: "Odile Hartwell looks after the Fenneldell Row bed".
- Hop item: M grows beans in bed X; M later moved the beans from X to Y; "M has gone away on a course, so Odile has M's

- Lures: M's old bed X; an earlier stand-in bed whose end is told in a later record naming only that gardener ("B is

### (b) Weaknesses targeted
- v1: number-keyed multi-hop chains, digit-swap twins, cancellations that must be unwound, and reasoning from absence
  ("never changed").
- v13: cover/hand-over binding (the item is stated only under another person), the relocation of that item, revocation
  stated in a separate record without the bed's name, and plans versus facts.

### (c) Version history
- v1 kept 8 families in code, but the shipped mix was reduced to relay and unch. Comments give the reasons:
  - "two hops were usually unsolved" (`generate.go:79`);
  - "3 lots were solved by all three in 8 of 12 cases" (`generate.go:249-251`);
  - "scenario 5 was unsolved 3 of 3" (`generate.go:262`);

- v13 is a full rewrite. All the v1 families were deleted (`t1.diff`). The dial history v12, v12s, v12t, v12L (a
  "ladder" of rungs), v13A-F appears as named settings with "pilot 4/5 (11-22)" and "lab/decisions.md" references
  (`stand.go:51-81`).
- The haystack shrank from about 1,500 pairs to 65 episodes (cases plus background) and 90 chatter lines
  (`stand.go:436-455`).

### (d) Difficulty tuning
- Shares are exact, not per-case draws: they are apportioned over the pack (`stand.go:128-195`).
- Explicit target: "z target about 0.3 per agent (band 0.2-0.4; our column usually has 4 eligible agents)"
  (`stand.go:52`). This reasons about the owner's own column with its own agent masked.
- The ladder keeps hard rungs at 15% "so that unsolved <= 0.15 even if the whole panel fails every hard rung"
  (`stand.go:59`).
- Count words are stated in 70% of questions. K3 (three-item) cases are capped at 25% and are off in v13.

### (e) Scorer-gaming signals
- Mild. The setup is the standard easy-half-plus-hard-half two-item list.
- In v1, the comment "No in-process validation ... would eat the 60 s budget" (`main.go:70-74`) explains why the
  generator skips the self-check.
- No question/gold mismatch was found. Distractors are provable from the cited records.

### (f) Shared code and borrowing
- Own codebase: templates are `K(...)` Kinds that an audit package parses back (`families/tmpl.go:1-8`). It is not a
  starter fork.
- The digit-swap twin idea is shared with tessera v2 (11-16) and staged-records, which dittogen cites
  (`dittogen:families/families.go:177`).
- Wrengen2's relay lure mirrors v1's relay.

### (f) Shared code

## 4. sn118-recall (5HWFP48f). Live v7 0.243

### (a) Case shapes
**v1** (b897a6bd, no meta). A starter-kit fork: the builder, main and pools follow the starter layout. Mix
(`families/families.go:279`):
- OwnerEmailCurrent: codename -> owner -> nickname -> move -> bounce correction;

- RevertOne (starter Revert);
- OfficeMidRange (starter DatedStatus anchored on a colleague's move).
- It was near-trivial on 11-16 (`edge_tracks.md:36`).

**v2** = "v3.5" (67d07e83, 11-20). "Venue lists" for a role:
- "Which cafes does my vet in Alderwick Arcade near Brackenmere go to now?"
- K=2 or K=3 gold venues (50/50), reached by:
  - R-role ("My vet in ... has coffee at X");
  - R-full (the new holder's full name, after "Old retired; from now on New is my vet in ...");
  - R-resumed (visit, then implicit quit "since X changed owners, New has not set foot there", then "New is back at X").
- Decoys: the old holder's venue, and a separate implicit-quit (M6) venue (`v2:families/weights.go:43`).

**v3** = "v3.16d" (cf7e87bf, 11-22):
- K=4 on 100% of cases (`families/weights.go:44`), adding R-title ("New uses the title Dr. Last" plus "Dr. Last has
  coffee at Y", `hidden.go:182-188`).
- The M6 decoy is dropped. Its RNG draws are kept, so the stream is unchanged (`hidden.go:162-168`).
- Only question templates 14 and 15 are used: "Which two cafes does %s use?" and "Based on what I told you, which two
  cafes does %s frequent?" (`weights.go:52`, `pools.go:44-45`).

### (b) Weaknesses targeted
- Role-to-person binding after a hand-over (the old holder's venue is the trap).
- Title and surname aliases ("Dr.").
- Implicit departure followed by a return.
- Look-alike venue names: all are three-word compositions with a domain suffix (`pools.go:15-26`).

### (c) Version history
- v1 -> v2: replaced starter-like single-value cases, which were near-trivial, with role-addressed venue lists.
- v2 -> v3: K=4 plus the "which two" wording. The README states the intent: "They ask for two venues; AnswerItems
  retains all four", and its matrix tests "K=4 singles (.25), all pairs (.5)" (`README.md:22-26, 46-49`).

### (d) Difficulty tuning
- Done through the question/gold mismatch rather than lures.
- An agent that answers the question as asked (two correct venues) scores exactly 0.5, which counts as solved. Only an
  agent that over-answers with all four gets 1.0. Naming the old holder's venue gives 0.
- So almost every case is "solved but not by everyone fully", with a mean near 0.5, and trivial cases need agents that
  ignore the question's count.

### (e) Scorer-gaming signals
- **Strongest in this group.** The question asks for two items while the gold holds four (`hidden.go:228` plus
  `weights.go:52`).
  - This caps correct behaviour at 0.5 and guarantees credit of about 0.5 per non-failing case.
  - It rewards agents that disobey the count, and it says nothing about memory.
  - It probably passes the validator because the four golds are all in evidence.
- Also: RNG draws are deliberately retained for removed content, so earlier calibration stays byte-stable
  (`hidden.go:162-168`).
- The README mentions external tooling (`out/verify.py`, `v3stats`) and testing against the official `grade.Memory`.

### (f) Shared code
- v1 is a starter fork (main and builder skeleton; Revert, Custody, DatedStatus analogues).
- From v2 on it is its own package (`internal/generator`, check/structure).
- The "Dr. <surname>" title alias converges with wrengen2's "Mr./Ms. <surname>" (Trap) and kinlist v8's FormTitle
  (`change.go:83-104`).

## 5. lh2gen (5EyKrm2f v4; 5CCrBmKy for the 11-22 build; owner runs agent lh2mem). Live v4 0.232 (0.299 on 11-18)

### (a) Case shapes
"Comparison threads" (`main.go:1-13`, `versus.go:9-115`). Mix: insuring 22, printing 22, hauling 21, quoting 5

  - one from its first quote;
  - one only after a revision. The revision is a bare "X sent a revised quote: N euros", buried in a "long note" of
    41-45 everyday sentences, at least 10 sentences from either end (`pools.go:259-267`, `builder.go:77-84`).
- A third firm beat the reference and was revised above it. A fourth never did, or a look-alike thread exists.
- The question never says how many.

### (b) Weaknesses targeted
- Numeric comparison against a reference after revisions; stale figures.
- Facts buried in long records. The long notes come to about 2.6-2.85k characters (55 musings, average 63 characters;
  estimate). That is just over the 2,500-character limit beyond which crown-v228 follows no name edges, and close to
  per-record packet budgets.
- Revisions that name the firm but not the thread.
- From the 11-22 build, a relative figure: "X came back with a new quote: 120 euros less than the latest quote from Y",
  where Y is the firm whose own revision moved it above the reference. This requires holding both revisions and doing
  arithmetic (`lh2gen_cfafd6bf:versus.go:74-91, 148-150`; `pools.go:275-281` RelativeShare=100).

### (c) Version history
- v8 (11-20) -> v16 (11-22, other hotkey). The only change is the relative-figure revision (100% where wording exists)
  and one pool word (`l1.diff`).
- `AliasShare=0`: "measured against released agents, such threads split them less often" (`pools.go:268-273`).
- `families.go` and `lists.go` keep about 1,000 lines of older families (Quotes, WeighIn, Waiting, Standings, Threshold,
  Kin, Keeper, Unanswered, Holders...). These are dead code apart from `Chatter` and `several`.

### (d) Difficulty tuning
- The easy item, from the first quote, gives 0.5 (solved).
- The hard item needs the buried revision. In v16 it also needs a second revision and arithmetic.
- Lures (the stale-cheaper firm, the look-alike thread) give 0.
- In-process validation with case replacement (`main.go:78-111`).

### (e) Scorer-gaming signals
- `Chatter` seeds one fixed note, "There is no oat milk left again." The comment explains it: reciting all of memory
  repeats "there is no", which the grader reads as declining to answer, so a memory dump is refused for every case at
  once (`families.go:782-787`).
  - This exploits the grader's abstention parser to defeat dump answers cheaply.
  - The comment's motive is validator time.
- The long-note length looks tuned to agent record-size limits more than to natural text. It is a defensible edge, but
  code-limit driven.
- **Own-agent alignment.** On 11-16 only lh2mem topped 0.6 on this column (`edge_tracks.md:32`). Long-note fact
  extraction and comparison reasoning are evidently lh2mem's strengths.

### (f) Shared code
- Own code. The two hotkeys ship the same tree: only main.go `Version`, pools.go and versus.go differ (`l1.diff`, 136
  lines). So it is the same owner, or a direct copy.

- **Old policies only.** It declares only grading policies v13 and v14 (`main.go:29`), unlike every other family here.

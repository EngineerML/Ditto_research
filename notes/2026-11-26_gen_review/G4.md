### (a) Case shapes and example
- **AliasPairList** (v17-v19; `unnamed-v5_c78f1254/src/families/families.go:20-44`). Example: "Which two bakeries does
  Jane Okafor love?" One record says "Jane Okafor loves Crumbhaven Bakery." A separate alias record binds Jane to
  "Midge", and "Midge adores Larkfield Bakehouse." Two look-alike nicknames one suffix away (Midgeo, Midgesy) are bound
  to other people and love same-frame bakeries. A same-surname relative's bakery sits in a sentence that names Jane.
- **AliasTripleList** (v23/24; `5DWjUMSx_1ba32b5d/src/families/families.go:54`): three items: direct, alias, initials
  alias ("W.J."). The question adds "Name every one you can find, even if it is fewer than three."
- **AliasTripleHidden** (v26-28; `unnamed-v4_da5215fd/src/families/families.go:140`): no direct item. All three items
  sit behind a nickname or initials, so solving needs two resolutions.
- **PairCorrectedAlias / PairCorrected** (v34-v38; `unnamed-v8_d8a96d1d/src/families/families.go:295,322`):
  two-item list. The easy item is under the full name. The hard item is first stated wrongly, then corrected in a later
  wave ("Correction on Midge: I had it wrong earlier, the place they actually love is X"), and the corrected-away
  value is a declared distractor. Each case adds 4+2 "chatter" notes naming the person
  (`builder.go:137`, `Chatter`).
- **PairBridge** (gen-bravo b44; `unnamed_815a249a/src/families/families.go:26-45`). "Which two coffee shops does
  P love?" One item is direct. The other needs two notes: "P is a regular at the coffee shop next to the crooked clock
  tower" plus a person-free note, "The coffee shop by the crooked clock tower is called X". Decoys are a same-surname
  relative's place and a "keeps saying they'll try X one day" place.
- **TablePair** (gen-charlie c43; `unnamed_1958bfaa/src/families/families.go:28-54`). A 4-slot routine table
  (weekday/weekend x morning/evening), a rota note, and a later move of the rota to the other time of day. A 2-slot
  company table plus "goes out with colleagues these days". Gold = post-move slot + company slot. Declared: the pre-move
  slot, a declined invitation, and the other company slot on even rounds (`distract`, :59-64).
- **MarkPair** (gen-delta d48; `unnamed_ea03b974/src/families/families.go:191-214`): "Where are the two things lent
  out at Ana's barbecue now?" Thing A's move names thing and event. Thing B's move names only a mark ("The one with the
  dented lid ended up at X"), and a separate note says "Ana's flask is the one with the dented lid". A stale note
  says "the flask is still at Y". On even rounds the stale Y is declared; on odd rounds another person's marked thing's
  new place is declared (:207-210).
- Unreleased intermediates documented in comments: d46 `TicketRoute` (:119, ticket → look-alike 4-digit shelf code →
  item) and gd47 `MarkTrio` (:157).

### (b) Agent weaknesses targeted
Nickname/alias binding and look-alike name tokens (v17-v28). Initials that a capitalised-word regex misses. Supersession
by a later correction (v34+). Multi-record joins where the deciding note shares no words with the question (landmark,
mark, table/rota) and retrieval keyed on person or question words misses it. Count pressure ("which two") that tempts
agents to fill the second slot with the stale or decoy value. Padding with other people's places, punished by
`CrossDistractors`.

- x6/x6b (11-08): three-item lists, because under near-perfect panels "the two-item list's direct half (1/2) made most
  cases trivial". x6b adds the partial-list licence after "abstentions ... on 15 of 20 cases from drpresident-v33"

- x6c/x6d (11-10): no direct item, because dan0l v46 resolved one nickname on 116/140 but both only on 18

  frontier oracle's bounded context ... always shows whose nickname each look-alike is" (:157-159).
- x6e (11-13): FileTriple (digit-swapped file numbers, `builder.go:117`) was tried, then dropped because "the twin trap

- x6g3 (11-16): `CrossDistractors(5)` (`unnamed-v7_017d774a/src/families/builder.go:182`, called `main.go:66`)

- 11-19 "one design per key (user 11-19)" (README of 815a249a / 1958bfaa / ea03b974). The modules were renamed

### (d) Difficulty tuning
The design swings between an easy-floor pair (risk: trivial) and no floor (risk: unsolved). It settled on an "easy half
+ rarely-found half + a declared trap for agents that fill the slot". charlie declares the other company slot only on
even rounds to move half-right answers to 0 (`unnamed_1958bfaa/.../families.go:56-64`). delta alternates the stale
place and the cross-person decoy by round parity (`unnamed_ea03b974/.../families.go:207-210`). Look-alike binding
records and all deciding notes are cited so that panel-unsolved cases can still be attested by the frontier oracle
(x6d note; PairBridge :22-24; TablePair :25-26).

### (e) Gaming vs real test
Mostly real retrieval and reference tests, steered hard by scoring mechanics:
- (i) The salt-per-sibling design evades fingerprint dedup across the operator's own keys (`main.go:30-31`).
- (ii) `CrossDistractors` and parity-declared decoys turn answer style (padding, filling a slot vs leaving it open)
  into zeros. MarkPair says explicitly that the top designs "zero by agent style (per-agent zero rate 0.0-1.0)" and
  that the full answer is rare (:186-190). That is discrimination by answering policy more than by memory.
- (iii) Evidence citation is engineered for the oracle's bounded context. PairBridge is built so that the oracle "joins
  them at once" while agents' retrieval "never retrieves the naming note". This is a legitimate multi-hop retrieval
  test, but one tuned to the oracle-vs-agent asymmetry.

## 1b. rollcall (5E7VQE3H, coldkey 5GZ3Xsu7v4; module `drpresident/sn118-rollcall{4,6}`)
- (a) Each case is a group roster with a per-member state (dues, consent forms, loaner kits, ten domains). The question
  names the group, not a person, and gives no count: "Who in the Thornbury judo club still owes this year's dues?"
  (README v6). In v4 (dd2c043d) a stayer is read off the tally and a joiner is moved in by a note "under their first
  name alone". The members to exclude were moved off by `post` (the note names a post, a separate note says who holds
  it), `place`, or a `rebound` (`unnamed_dd2c043d/src/roll/families.go:199`). Mix of 1-3 such members per case
  (:417). v6 (41fc50c3) adds a **group-wide sweep**: "all the outstanding dues have come in ..." settles everyone
  (`unnamed_41fc50c3/src/roll/families.go:165`). Then two members go open again (one tagged with the group, one by
  first name), and in `rc6-l` one more goes open and then leaves the group for good (`gone`, :161; Mix :517, 9 of 12
  slots).
- (b) Targets state tracking over time, aggregate (group-level) updates applied to individuals, first-name and post
  coreference, and membership changes. Every other member is declared, as are same-state members of other groups.
- (c) v4 → v6: dropped post/place/rebound traps and roles (`roleNotes` removed). Added sweep/early/back/left shapes; only
  rc6-l and rc6-s are dealt.
- (d) Two gold members per case. One sits on the easy side in v4 (the stayer from the tally); v6 makes both depend on
  post-sweep notes. No explicit floor in v6, so the unsolved risk is higher. Board 07ab3a0f (v9) 0.102, with per-round
  results 0.106-0.385.
- (e) A clean state-tracking test with an unambiguous record design ("every record has one owner", README). Gaming is
  limited to declaring all non-answers as distractors.
